You are writing the salvage material table for Breaker-Yard, a 2D top-down orbital ship-breaking simulation
(think Hardspace: Shipbreaker crossed with RimWorld). A crew cuts derelict ships apart with torches. Chunks that
come loose drift free as debris, and the player grapples them into one of three salvage bins:

- furnace: melts down bulk ferrous scrap
- processor: reclaims structural alloys and machined parts for reuse
- barge: takes intact, high-value components to be resold whole

Each tile of a ship is made of one material. A chunk delivered into a bin pays each tile's value if the tile
belongs in that bin, and only 25% of its value if it does not, so players want to cut chunks into pure pieces.
Mass decides how hard a chunk is to grapple and how hard it hits things.

Write exactly these 5 materials, one entry each, with these ids:
- deck-plate: the floor plating inside the ship (by far the most common tile)
- hull-plate: interior bulkhead walls
- girder-alloy: the load-bearing frame girders
- engine-part: tiles of the engine block bolted to the frame
- electronics: consoles and control equipment

The demo ship contains 915 deck-plate, 24 hull-plate, 115 girder-alloy, 13 engine-part and 6 electronics tiles.

Rules (an automatic check rejects anything that breaks them):
- id: exactly one of the five ids above
- name: 3-24 characters, a short in-world label (e.g. "Ferrous Deck Plate")
- description: 20-120 characters, one plain sentence of in-world flavour, no emoji
- value: whole number of credits per tile, 1-500
- mass: number from 0.2 to 10, in girder units (a plain girder tile is about 1)
- bin: "furnace", "processor" or "barge"; every bin must receive at least one material
- color: lowercase "#rrggbb", all five different, readable on a near-black background
- mass rises deck-plate <= hull-plate <= girder-alloy, and engine-part is the heaviest of all
- engine-part and electronics are each worth at least 3x the per-tile value of every plate and girder material
- the demo ship, sorted perfectly, pays between 6000 and 20000 credits in total (multiply each value by its tile
  count above and add them up; check your arithmetic)
- no single bin may take more than 60% of that sorted total, so sorting matters

Answer with only a JSON array of 5 objects with exactly these fields, like:
