You are designing derelict ships for Breaker-Yard, a 2D top-down orbital ship-breaking simulation (Hardspace:
Shipbreaker crossed with RimWorld). Each ship hangs off a docking gantry by two arms on its LEFT side. A crew of up
to three walks its decks with cutting torches; the player cuts the ship into pieces and grapples them into three
salvage bins (furnace, processor, barge). Each ship comes with a salvage contract: deliver a credit quota before
the clock runs out.

Write {{count}} NEW ships as a JSON array. Already shipped (do not reuse these ids or copy these designs): {{existing}}.
Make every ship a different kind of vessel with its own layout, e.g. an ore hauler, a fuel tanker, a survey
cutter, a passenger shuttle, a mining tender, a patrol corvette, a cargo lighter, a medical frigate.

COORDINATES. Each ship is a grid of width x height tiles. (0, 0) is the top-left tile; x grows right, y grows down.
The outer ring of tiles (x = 0, x = width-1, y = 0, y = height-1) is the load-bearing frame, built automatically.
Everything inside it starts as walkable deck. Lines are [x1, y1, x2, y2], inclusive, and must be horizontal or
vertical. Rects are [x, y, w, h].

FIELDS
- id: kebab-case, at most 32 chars. name: 3-32 chars. description: 20-160 chars, one in-world sentence.
- width: 16-38. height: 12-40.
- arms: the two rows (1 to height-2) on the left frame where the gantry arms hold the ship, at least 4 apart.
- bulkheads: interior walls, lines strictly inside the frame (x 1..width-2, y 1..height-2). They split the deck
  into compartments.
- doors: [x, y] tiles ON a bulkhead that become openings, so the crew can walk between compartments.
- girders: interior load-bearing walls, lines strictly inside the frame. They block movement like bulkheads (doors
  cannot go through them) but carry load to the arms. Use them to stiffen long or heavy ships. May be [].
- engine: one line ON the frame ring, NOT on the left side (x = 0 is docked). 3-16 tiles. Very heavy and valuable.
- consoles: solid electronics rects strictly inside the frame, 2-20 tiles in total. Valuable.
- atmosphere: [x, y, bar] on any deck tile sets the pressure of everything connected to it through doors (0-6
  bar), so give pressure pockets their own sealed room. Everything not listed holds 1 bar. Over 1.5 bar is an overpressure hazard (crews refuse to cut walls holding
  back more than 2 bar); under 0.2 bar is a vacuum pocket.
- leaks: up to 3 [x, y] deck tiles that seep toxic gas.
- crew: 1-3 [x, y] deck tiles, all in the same compartment, where the crew starts.
- contract: {"client": 3-30 chars, "brief": 20-200 chars, one or two sentences telling the crew what matters,
  "quota": whole credits, "timeLimit": 240-900 seconds}.

RULES (an automatic check builds every ship in the simulator and rejects anything that breaks them)
- 2-12 rooms, each at least 4 deck tiles. A room is a deck area bounded by walls, with doors counted as walls, so
  bulkheads with doors in them divide rooms. Doors must sit on bulkhead tiles (not girders, consoles or deck).
  Air flows through doors: a room with no door is a sealed compartment, the only way to hold a different pressure.
- From the first crew tile the crew must be able to walk (through doors) to at least 50% of all deck tiles, and to
  a deck tile next to every console and next to the engine.
- 1-4 hazards in total: compartments over 1.5 bar, compartments under 0.2 bar, and leaks. At most 2 overpressure.
- The frame must hold the ship's own weight: the ship is a cantilever off its left arms, so a long ship with a heavy
  engine far from the arms overloads the frame. Peak member load must stay under 85% of breaking. Spread the arms
  apart, keep long ships' engines shorter, or add interior girders running from the left frame toward the engine.
- Value: each tile is worth credits when delivered to the right bin: deck 4, bulkhead 6, frame or girder 10,
  engine 100, console 80. The whole ship must be worth 2500-15000 credits, and no single bin may hold over 75% of it
  (deck goes to the furnace; bulkheads and girders to the processor; engine and consoles to the barge).
- contract.quota must be 25%-60% of the ship's total value.
- contract.timeLimit must suit the job: about 200 seconds plus 0.5 seconds per deck tile, within 30% either way
  (and 240-900). A small hull with 200 deck tiles gets about 300 s; a big one with 900 gets about 650 s.

HOW TO WORK. Do not verify the rules by hand: the check computes the exact value, frame load and crew reach and
reports the numbers when something fails, and you will get the chance to fix exactly what it names. Design the
ship, estimate its value roughly (deck tiles x 4 is most of it, plus 100 per engine tile and 80 per console tile,
plus about 10 per frame tile), set the quota near 40% of that estimate and the time limit from the deck size, and answer. Keep your thinking short.

SIZE. Prefer mid-size and large hulls (width 26-38, height 18-36): small hulls are already in the fleet.
