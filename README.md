# Breaker-Yard

Systemic 2D orbital salvage simulator (TypeScript + Vite + Canvas 2D). Project rules live in `CLAUDE.md`.

```sh
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173
npm test           # unit tests, including the zero-allocation tick check
npm run build      # strict typecheck + production bundle
npm run gen -- materials           # regenerate the material table with the local model
npm run gen -- ships --count 2     # generate more ships (validated, repaired, merged by id)
```

## Phase 1 layout

## Models in brief

**Gas.** Each tick first runs 8 flow substeps: every open face carries a signed flux (`flowX`, `flowY`) that keeps 90% of its momentum and gains 0.25 per bar of pressure difference across the face, and a limiter stops any cell giving away more gas than it holds. Then it runs 8 diffusion substeps of `p' = p + k * sum(p_n - p)` with `k = 0.24` (stable below 0.25). Walls are zero-flux, vacuum is pinned at 0 bar and only receives, and gas drained into vacuum is tallied in `vacuumAbsorbed`, so `totalMass() + vacuumAbsorbed` is conserved. A one-tile breach empties about 40% of the 1 bar demo room in 5 s and nearly all of it in 35 s. The flow gain and damping are clamped where the flow + diffusion tick stays stable; past them (for example 95% momentum) a sealed room slowly rings up from float rounding.

**Vent impulse.** `getVentImpulse` is the negative pressure gradient plus the gas stream through the cell (0.5 per bar of flux per tick). At a 1 bar breach it reads about 2.2 bar/tile in the hole, 1.5 one tile in and 0.6 two tiles in, so magboots slip near the hole; the jet just outside reads about 1.9, which overpowers an adrift pawn's thrusters and carries it away from the hull. Pawn speed is capped at 15 tiles/s per axis so a hard blowout cannot tunnel a pawn through a wall.

**Stress.** Each girder's mass has to reach an anchor. The solver finds a load potential with anchors at 0 (`deg * phi - sum(phi_n) = mass`), and an edge carries `phi_a - phi_b`. A cantilever segment therefore carries exactly the mass beyond it, and parallel members share load. Nodes cut off from every anchor are marked floating. Thresholds are only checked once the solve has converged, so a half-relaxed solution never snaps members.

**Toxic gas.** `GasGrid.toxin` is a toxic partial pressure that rides inside the bulk gas: diffusion runs it through the same kernel, and flow carries it upwind as the donor cell's toxic share of each face flux, so it vents with the gas and is conserved the same way (`totalToxin() + toxinVented`). `HAZARD_LEAK` tiles now emit toxic gas.

**Path costs.** Walls and vacuum are 0xFFFF. Floor costs 10, plus 40 per bar of local pressure differential (max minus min over the cell and its open or vacuum neighbours), plus 400 per bar of toxin, plus 100 to 600 on the 3x3 block around any member above 80% utilisation.

**Pawns.** A pawn claims the highest-priority, nearest order its tools cover, walks to a tile next to the wall, and scans it for 0.5 s (PRE_CUT). If the wall is holding back more than 2.0 bar (`GasGrid.getPressureDelta`) the cut is refused, logged, and the order is suspended for 10 s. While cutting, the torch softens a girder's members (up to half their capacity) and the pawn keeps re-checking the differential; at 100% the tile becomes floor and its stress node is removed. Vent impulse drags pawns; locked magboots absorb up to 6 tiles/s² of it. A pawn that spends 3 ticks in a stream above 0.15 bar/tile, or is knocked off the floor, drops its work and flees to a floor tile braced against a wall, away from the stream, where it clamps until things are calm. Adrift pawns thrust back to the nearest floor.

**Zero allocation.** All float maths in the pawn tick lives in `tickPawn`, which runs for every pawn every tick and so stays optimised; the per-state functions are integer-only, because they are cold enough to run in V8's interpreter, where double arithmetic allocates. Doubles cross calls through a `Float64Array` rather than as arguments. `alloc.test.ts` checks the world tick sealed, venting, snapping and venting toxin, and the full simulation tick with pawns working, refusing and fleeing.

**Tearing free.** Whenever the lattice rebuilds with floating nodes, or a tile disappears, the world flood-fills its tiles. Neighbours hold together unless both are girders and the member between them has snapped, so frames part where members break and decks and bulkheads go with whatever they are welded to. Every group with no grounded node is lifted out of the grid into a rigid body, along with its materials, the air inside it (`gasCarried`, so `totalMass() + vacuumAbsorbed + gasCarried` is conserved) and a push from any gas venting through it. A map with no anchors at all never tears. The torch only leaves floor where a cut tile borders a deck, so a cut gantry arm really parts. Cutting one arm of the demo hull overloads the other, which shatters, and the 1,079-tile hull floats off with its crew aboard: riders move with the wreck and float free at its velocity when it is delivered.

**Debris.** Bodies are tile offsets from a mass-weighted centre, with position, angle, velocity and spin, and inertia summed over tiles. Contacts are sampled at each tile's centre and four inset corners against grid tiles, the map edge and cells stamped by other bodies. Each one applies an impulse at the contact point (restitution 0.2, friction 0.4, which gives spin) and removes 80% of the penetration per tick, so a tile stops at a wall's face (at most one tick of travel past it). Speed is capped at 8 tiles/s, and spin so that the outermost tile stays under 8 tiles/s, which keeps contacts from tunnelling. The grapple is a critically damped spring per unit mass, limited to 150 force: a deck chunk snaps to the cursor, while the hull (mass ~680) creeps.

**Salvage.** Each tile has a material (deck plate, hull plate, girder alloy, engine part, electronics) with a value, a mass and the bin it belongs in. Debris with every tile inside a bin is consumed: right-bin tiles pay full value, the rest 25%. The bins are smaller than the hull, so it has to be cut apart, and a torch cut destroys the value of the tile it removes. The material table is generated by the local model behind `materialTableErrors`, which also prices the demo hull: 6,000-20,000 credits sorted, at most 60% in any one bin, and engine parts and electronics worth at least 3x any plating.

**Zero allocation, debris.** Drifting and towing (contacts, impulses, the grapple) allocate nothing per tick. Building a body is a one-off event that runs too rarely to stay optimised: V8 drops the optimised per-tile code once the warm-up world is collected, so a tear costs about 640 B per lifted tile, once. `alloc.test.ts` budgets it per tile and holds the ticks around it to the per-tick limit.

**Ships.** A blueprint is a compact JSON description: hull size, interior bulkheads and girders as lines, doors, an engine line on the frame, console rects, compartment pressures, toxic leaks, gantry arm rows, crew start tiles and the contract. `buildShip` docks it at the yard's fixed berth. `shipErrors` checks the shape, then builds the ship and runs it for 10 seconds. The frame must hold its own weight with peak member load under 85%, and the hull must not leak. It needs 2-10 compartments of at least 4 tiles, and the crew must reach half the deck plus every console and the engine. It needs 1-4 hidden hazards (overpressure, vacuum pockets, leaks), a sorted value of 2,500-15,000 credits with no bin over 75%, and a quota of 25-60% of that value. Every message names the tile or number that failed, which the generator feeds back to the model to repair.

**Contracts.** `N` docks the next ship in the fleet and starts its contract. The clock runs with the simulation. The contract closes when time runs out or when you press `N`, and is graded on credits delivered against quota: C at quota, B at 1.2x, A at 1.5x, F below quota.

**Crew hauling.** Every pawn has a priority for each job, cut and haul, from 1 (first) to 4 (last) or off; an idle pawn takes the first job kind, in priority order, that has work. A salvage designation (Ctrl+drag) places SEVER orders on the ring around the rectangle: the crew cuts each ring tile away completely (a wall to deck, then the deck to vacuum), reaching across corners when a tile has no 4-neighbour left to stand on, until the block comes loose. A hauler then picks the nearest loose chunk that is light enough (up to 150), free, and has a bin to go to; walks to a deck tile within 2.5 tiles of it; climbs aboard; and pushes it with its suit thrusters (a force-limited spring, 60 force) out to the haul lane at x = 88, along it, and into the bin that pays most for that chunk's materials. When the bin takes it, the pawn flies back via the lane to the tile it left from. A tow that gains no ground for 8 seconds is abandoned, and the chunk is left alone for 30 seconds.

**Zero allocation, hauling.** The crew's approach, tug and return code allocates nothing per tick: `alloc.test.ts` samples every allocation during a haul and charges it to the function that made it, and only setup and the once-per-tow steps (boarding, letting go) may show up in `PawnManager`. Two existing kernel issues the haul exposed are fixed: `diffuse` read cell types as property loads inside branches, and the vent tallies were number fields whose representation changed as gas vented; both kept invalidating optimised code. One is left: while a single small chunk is all there is, V8 keeps re-tiering `flow`, `diffuse` and `DebrisField.step` (about 10 bytes per tick of churn, not garbage from any one site), so the haul test logs the total rather than holding it to the per-tick limit.
